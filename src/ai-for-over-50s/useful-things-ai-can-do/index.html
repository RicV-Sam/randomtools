15 useful things AI can help you do

AI is most useful when it helps with a task you already understand. These examples begin with drafts, ideas and organisation—jobs where you can recognise a helpful answer and correct a poor one.

A sensible first use

Choose something low risk and easy to judge

Begin with a familiar task whose result you can check yourself. A draft invitation is a better first experiment than a question about medication, tax or a legal dispute. Leave names and identifying details out until you have copied the draft into your own document.

The useful part is not always the final answer

AI can help you move past a blank page, arrange scattered thoughts or suggest questions. You remain responsible for selecting, checking and editing the result.

Writing and communication

Create a first draft you can make your own

  1. Draft a friendly email.Describe the purpose, recipient and tone, then replace placeholders with real details after leaving the AI chat.
  2. Make a message shorter.Ask for the same meaning in fewer words, then check that no important qualification disappeared.
  3. Turn notes into a clear list.Paste non-sensitive notes and ask for headings, actions and unanswered questions.
  4. Prepare for a phone call.Ask for an opening sentence and five questions about the general topic.

Planning and organising

Give a rough idea some useful structure

  1. Build a packing checklist.Include the length, season and general activities, but keep booking references and passport details out.
  2. Create a simple meal plan.State the number of people, ordinary preferences and ingredients to avoid; check medical dietary needs independently.
  3. Break a project into steps.Ask for the first five actions, likely obstacles and materials you may need.
  4. Compare two everyday options.Choose the criteria yourself—such as cost, effort and convenience—and verify changing prices.

Learning and hobbies

Ask for an explanation, example and practice

  1. Explain an unfamiliar term.Ask for plain English, one everyday example and a short definition of each new word.
  2. Practise a language.Request a short conversation at your level, one sentence at a time, with gentle corrections.
  3. Generate hobby ideas.Set a budget, time limit and available materials so the suggestions fit real life.
  4. Create a small quiz.Use it to check what you understood, then ask why any answer was wrong.

Reviewing and checking

Use a second pass to expose gaps

  1. Find unclear parts of your draft.Ask what a reader might misunderstand rather than asking the AI to praise it.
  2. List assumptions in a plan.Ask which facts, costs, dates or dependencies still need confirmation.
  3. Turn an answer into a checking list.Separate factual claims from suggestions and identify the primary source that could confirm each fact.

Do not hand over high-stakes decisions

For health, law, money, safety or major purchases, use AI only to organise questions or understand general language. Verify the substance with a qualified professional or authoritative current source.
