How to ask AI follow-up questions

The first answer does not need to be perfect. Stay in the same conversation, say what is missing or wrong, and ask for one useful change at a time.

See six useful follow-upsFollow an example
{% include "partials/editorial-trust.njk" %}

Why follow up?

An AI chat remembers the earlier messages in that conversation

You normally do not need to repeat the entire request. You can refer to “the second option”, “that list” or “the email above”. A good follow-up tells the AI what to keep, what to change and what a better result would look like.

A simple pattern

Keep [the useful part], change [the problem], and make the result [the outcome you want].

Useful phrases

Six kinds of follow-up question

1. Make it clearer

Explain that again in plain English. Define any technical terms and use one everyday example.

2. Change the tone

Keep the meaning, but make it warmer and less formal. Avoid exaggerated or sales-like language.

3. Add useful detail

Add the first three practical steps. For each one, say what I need and what could go wrong.

4. Make it shorter

Reduce this to 100 words. Keep the date, requested action and important limitation.

5. Show alternatives

Give me three different options: simplest, lowest cost and most flexible. Explain one drawback of each.

6. Expose uncertainty

Which statements in your answer are uncertain, may have changed or need checking? Suggest a primary source for each one.

Worked conversation

Improve an invitation in three small turns

  1. 01Start: “Write a friendly email inviting neighbours to a Saturday garden tidy-up at 10am. Keep it under 120 words.”
  2. 02Correct: “This sounds too formal. Make it more relaxed, but keep the time and purpose.”
  3. 03Complete: “Add one sentence asking people to bring gloves if they have them. Do not imply that attendance is expected.”

Each turn deals with one visible problem. That is easier to judge than asking the AI to “make it better”, because “better” does not explain what you value.

When to start again

Use a new chat when the task or privacy context changes

  • ✓Start a new chat when you move to an unrelated task and old context could confuse the answer.
  • ✓Start again if the conversation has become long, contradictory or fixed on the wrong assumption.
  • ✓Do not add private information merely because the first answer was vague; use placeholders or explain the general situation.
  • ✓If a factual answer matters, verify it outside the conversation even after a convincing correction.
