Wheel of Names for the classroom

A classroom wheel for projectors, whiteboards, quizzes, and student turns

picked
—

A classroom wheel of names helps teachers make random picking visible to the whole room. It works well on projectors, interactive whiteboards, and shared screens when you want student turns, group prompts, quiz choices, reward spins, or quick activity selection to feel open and easy to follow. This page stays classroom-specific so the examples support lessons, not broad giveaway or event use.

Why use a name wheel in class

  • Equity of voice — quiet students get called on as often as the hand-raisers
  • Reduced resistance — "the wheel picked you" is easier to accept than "I'm picking you"
  • Attention — students stay alert because anyone could be next
  • Pace — one tap, one answer, no waiting for hands
  • Visibility — the whole class can see the selection happen on the board

Three ways to use it well

1. The cold call. Ask a question, pause for thinking time, then tap the wheel. That keeps the routine fair and gives everyone a chance to prepare.

2. The quiz or activity picker. Add revision topics, challenge cards, or next-step activities so the wheel chooses what the class does next.

3. The turn or presentation order. For group work or individual presentations, spin the wheel once per slot to set the order live.

Projectors, groups, and quizzes

A classroom wheel is especially useful when the class needs to see the choice happen. Use it to pick who answers next, which team goes first, which revision topic appears, or which quiz challenge the room gets. On a whiteboard or large display, the visual spin helps the process feel shared rather than hidden.

Three common mistakes

  • Skipping the picked student — once the wheel calls a name, commit to it unless there is a genuine safeguarding or support reason not to.
  • Using it for high-stakes questions — keep the wheel for participation, games, revision, and routine class decisions.
  • Overusing it — save it for moments where visible choice improves the lesson rather than turning every decision into a spin.
