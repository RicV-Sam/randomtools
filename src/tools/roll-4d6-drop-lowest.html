Roll a full D&D 5e character stat block in one tap — four six-sided dice, drop the lowest, repeated six times for the six ability scores. The dropped die and the three kept dice are shown below every roll so you (and your DM) can verify the math. The standard Rolling Scores method from the 5e Player's Handbook, also used in Pathfinder and many OSR retroclones. Kinder than 3d6: average is 12.24, scores of 13+ show up more than half the time, so heroes actually feel heroic.

How to roll a 5e character

  1. Leave the count set to 6 — you need six ability scores.
  2. Tap ROLL STATS. Four dice are rolled six times; the lowest die from each set is dropped.
  3. Each of the six totals becomes an ability score.
  4. Assign them (in order, or freely if your DM allows) to Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma.
  5. Apply racial bonuses per your chosen species.

Is it a good roll?

A useful rule of thumb: the sum of all six scores should be at least 70 to be competitive. Under 65 is rough; over 80 is notably above average. Most DMs let you reroll once if the total is below a set minimum — ask before you start.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I roll a full D&D 5e stat block with 4d6 drop lowest?

Set the count to 6 and tap Roll Stats. The tool rolls four d6 six times, drops the lowest die from each set, and gives you six ability scores ready to assign to STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA.

What's a good 4d6 drop lowest total for a D&D character?

Aim for at least 70 across all six scores to feel competitive. Under 65 is rough; over 80 is well above average. Many DMs allow a single re-roll if your total is below an agreed floor.

What's the average 4d6 drop lowest?

12.24. Modal value is 13. An 18 appears about 1.6% of the time per roll, so expect roughly one 18 every ten characters on average.

4d6 drop lowest vs point buy — which is better for D&D 5e?

Rolling 4d6 drop lowest averages higher (~74 total) than 27-point buy but adds variance — you might roll a powerhouse or a dud. Point buy guarantees balance; rolling rewards luck. Ask your DM which is allowed at the table.

Can I see all four dice, not just the total?

Yes. After each roll, the dropped die and kept dice are shown below the score so you can verify the math.

Does this follow the 5e Player's Handbook rules?

Yes. This is the "Rolling Scores" method from the 5e PHB character creation chapter. Your DM may allow rerolls or use point-buy instead.
