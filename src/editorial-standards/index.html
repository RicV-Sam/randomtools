Who · How · Why

How Spinnit creates and checks AI learning guides

AI products and safety advice change quickly. This page explains who is responsible for Spinnit's beginner guidance, which sources we use and how we correct or update a page.

Responsibility

A small independent team remains accountable for every page

Spinnit's AI learning pages are created and checked by Spinnit Editorial, the small independent team behind the site. We do not present AI-generated text as an expert opinion, and no AI provider approves or controls these guides.

We currently use an organisational byline rather than inventing an individual expert identity. Questions and corrections reach the people responsible through hello@spinnit.site or the contact page.

Our method

Each guide begins with a real beginner task

  1. 01Define the outcome. The page must help a beginner understand or complete one useful task without unnecessary jargon.
  2. 02Check primary sources. Product controls, privacy behaviour, prices and official reporting routes are checked against the provider, regulator or public body responsible.
  3. 03Test the explanation. Steps, examples and links are reviewed for clarity, safety and whether a reader can act without hidden technical knowledge.
  4. 04State limits. Guides distinguish drafts and suggestions from facts, warn when information may change and direct important decisions to an appropriate professional or primary source.
  5. 05Publish a review date. Time-sensitive guides show when their changing details were last checked.

Evidence

Official sources come first

For product settings and behaviour, we prefer the provider's current help centre or documentation. For scams, reporting, privacy and public safety, we prefer the relevant government, regulator, police or national cybersecurity body. Important source links are listed on the guide where readers can check them directly.

Independent reviews can help identify questions, usability problems or competing interpretations. They do not replace verification against the live site and authoritative sources.

Use of AI

AI may assist the process; people make the editorial decisions

AI tools may be used to explore questions, compare possible structures, identify unclear wording or produce a draft for review. A human checks the resulting copy, links, claims, examples, metadata and page behaviour before publication. We do not publish large sets of automatically generated pages or change dates merely to make content look fresh.

Updates and corrections

A visible date means the changing details were checked

We review a page when a provider changes an interface or policy, an official reporting route changes, a material error is reported or the guide receives a substantive update. A review date is not a promise that every external service will remain unchanged after that day.

If something is wrong or out of date, email hello@spinnit.site with the page address, the affected passage and a source where possible. Material corrections are made in the page itself rather than hidden in a separate feed.

Boundaries

What these guides do not claim to provide

  • ✓They provide general education, not medical, legal, financial or cybersecurity incident advice for an individual case.
  • ✓They do not guarantee that an AI answer, provider or paid plan will be suitable for every person.
  • ✓They do not treat sponsorship, affiliate availability or provider popularity as proof of quality.
  • ✓They encourage readers to keep final judgement, verify important claims and use trusted professional help when the stakes are high.
