The 4 building blocks of a great prompt

Rather than thinking of a prompt as a single sentence, think of it as a small blueprint. High-performing prompts combine four structural building blocks:

1. Role / Persona

Tells the AI what perspective or expertise to adopt (e.g. "Act as an experienced copywriter" or "Act as a high school physics teacher").

2. Core Task

The primary action you want the AI to perform (e.g. "Draft an email", "Summarize this meeting transcript", or "Explain this formula").

3. Context & Background

The essential facts, source data, target audience, and background details the AI needs to give a relevant answer.

4. Output Specification

The desired structure, length, tone, or format (e.g. "3 bullet points", "Markdown table", or "under 150 words").

Putting the blocks together

Compare how a prompt evolves when you add each building block step by step:

Bare task (No structure)

Task only

Give me meal prep ideas for the week.

Generates generic recipes that may not suit your diet, cooking time, budget, or family size.

Full 4-Part Prompt

All 4 building blocks active

Act as a practical nutritionist [ROLE]. Recommend 5 weekday dinner prep ideas [TASK] for a busy family of four with a £70 weekly budget and a peanut allergy [CONTEXT]. Present the results in a 3-column table showing Meal Name, Cooking Time, and Main Ingredients [OUTPUT].

Removes ambiguity, respects dietary constraints, and produces a table ready to use immediately.

When to use all four blocks

You do not need to write a 4-part prompt when asking simple questions like "What is the capital of Canada?". However, whenever your task involves:

  • Writing professional emails, reports, or customer responses
  • Analyzing complex documents or datasets
  • Creating educational content or study guides
  • Generating code or structured data formats

Using all four building blocks ensures you get a tailored, structured response on the first try without wasting turns on back-and-forth edits.
