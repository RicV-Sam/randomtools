## What the learner should be able to do

Open ChatGPT, start a text conversation, ask for a useful change, check supplied facts, copy the result and start a fresh task. This first unit does not teach every ChatGPT feature, uploads, voice, professional advice or other platforms.

Allow about 20–30 minutes as an initial estimate, with breaks whenever needed. Actual completion time has not been measured with learners yet. Each person should use the device they normally use. No paid subscription is required by the tasks; platform access and sign-in requirements can vary.

## Before starting

Ask: “How comfortable would you feel using ChatGPT for a small everyday task on your own?” Let them answer in their own words. Note whether they have tried it before. Do not assume experience from age.

Use only the fictional coffee-morning and book-group details supplied in the lessons. Let the learner enter any necessary account credentials privately. Do not record their credentials, actual chat content or personal details.

If two people share one browser, leave “Remember my place” off and reload for each new learner. If it was enabled, untick it before reloading to remove this pilot's saved record. Progress does not move between devices.

## During the test

Let them operate the device. Ask them to say what they expect to happen. Do not teach ahead of the page. If they get stuck, offer help and note the exact step and what was unclear. They can stop at any time.

Each step offers optional Heart narration. Let them choose whether to listen or read. Note whether they can find play, pause, replay and the slower setting without help. Ask whether the voice is clear, whether any word sounds odd, and whether the pace helps them follow the task. Audio should stop when they change steps; quiz feedback can be played after checking an answer. Record difficulties with returning from ChatGPT while listening. Use headphones if helpful, and keep the learner in control of the volume. Check actual voice quality with listeners; automated playback checks cannot establish that the delivery feels natural or comfortable.

Record each task as **on my own**, **with help**, or **not yet**. “With help” includes a hint, a demonstration or another person's instructions. Do not turn these observations into a pass percentage.

## At the end

Ask:

1. What would you feel comfortable using ChatGPT for now?
2. Which part would you still want help with?
3. What would you do if an answer sounded convincing but included a detail you did not supply?
4. What useful task would you like the next lesson to teach?

Success for this pilot means the learner can complete a new low-risk conversation with less help, explain why facts need checking, and identify where to get help. Feeling confident is useful feedback, but compare it with what they actually managed to do. Do not promise confidence from completing the unit.

## Try again on another day

Ask for a short thank-you note to a fictional neighbour who watered the plants. Let the learner write the request without an example, ask for a change, and check that the answer added no gifts or promises. Record whether they need less help than before. If not, revise or repeat the step that caused difficulty.

Keep observations privately and share only the general difficulties and suggestions back with Ric. The pilot does not submit test feedback or confidence responses.
